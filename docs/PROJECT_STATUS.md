## What this project is

A deterministic, data-driven tactical RPG engine in the Troubleshooter /
Final Fantasy Tactics lineage, plus three authoring tools and one in-progress
campaign built on top of it. The simulation is an authoritative event queue
with a continuous recovery timeline; combat mechanics are composed from a
registry of ~33 generic effects, 11 target filters and 17 effect conditions
rather than from per-ability code. Nine phases of engine work have added
reactions, causality, perception, trajectories, propagation chains, battlefield
fixtures, pre-resolution interventions, activation sequencing and resource
transfer — each as a generic primitive with a content-only vertical slice
proving it. Authoring happens in three browser editors (missions/maps, scenes,
gameplay data) that write canonical JSON the game loads directly.

**Scale:** 65,500 lines of source, of which `src/App.jsx` alone is **44,656**.

**Derived at runtime, not authored:** maps (23) and encounters (30) — mostly
literals in `App.jsx` plus compiled mission files.

**Operator addressing is now single.** An operator entry used to carry both a
registry key and an optional `ref`, allowed to differ — and for one pilot they
did. The entry key is now the one canonical id everywhere: campaign roster,
deployment, mission unit refs, combat links, reaction ownership and scene
speakers. The `ref` field is gone from the schema and its presence is a
validation error, because a second identifier that agrees today is exactly how
the last one survived long enough to disagree. A save written before the change
is translated once, at load, and never again.

# Generic Tactical Primitives Already Available

## Movement
Normal pathfound movement · forced movement (push/pull/displace/teleport/swap)
· multi-segment trajectories with five priced redirect categories · approach-
distance scaling · `displacementResistance` · mid-path fixture interruption ·
voluntary-vs-forced distinction throughout.

## Targeting
Direct unit · tile · empty tile · self · ally/enemy/any filters · area shapes
(single, diamond, ring, line) · 11 target filters (`hasStatus`,
`missingStatus`, `hpBelowPercent`, `hpAbovePercent`, `unitTag`, `classTag`,
`terrainTag`, `summoned`, `notSummoned`, `sourceOnly`, `excludeSource`) ·
propagation chains with selection policies · fixture targeting by tag ·
activation-window targeting.

## Effects — 33 registered types
```
damage · heal · applyStatus · removeStatus · cleanse · dispel · copyStatus · transferStatus
shield · modifyStat · revive · transformUnit · spawnUnit · despawnUnit
moveUnit · displace · push · pull · teleport · swapPositions
restoreResource · spendResource · transferResource · refreshActivationResource
modifyTimeline · createTerrain · removeTerrain
placeFixture · setFixtureState
delayedEffect · conditionalEffect · repeatEffect · propagate
```
Every one has a handler, metadata, validator, forecaster and AI scorer —
enforced by the architecture audit.

## Conditions & triggers
**Effect conditions (18):** HP thresholds either side, status presence/absence,
alive/defeated, ally/enemy, tile empty, terrain tag, distance at most/least,
resource balance (either side, either scope, count or percent-of-max), chance.
**Reaction conditions (30):** relationship, range, knowledge state, line of
sight, became-exposed, status on reactor or subject, resource balance on the
reactor or either side of the event, forced-vs-voluntary, declaration kind,
redirectability, prevention reason, mission facts, and boolean
`all`/`any`/`not`.

**Status trigger moments (6):** `activationStart` · `activationEnd` ·
`unitDamaged` · `unitMoved` · `killedUnit` · `statusApplied`. Effects land on
the holder by default, or on the moment's counterpart where it has one. Two
further moments were evaluated and deliberately omitted — see
[`docs/TACTICAL_LANGUAGE.md`](TACTICAL_LANGUAGE.md).

**Effect scaling sources (7):** approach distance, segment distance, redirect
count, hop index, hop distance, propagation kills, target status. Each is a
count multiplied by an authored `perUnit`, in `power` or `multiplier` mode.
**Reaction triggers (15):** activation start/end, `actionDeclared`,
`actionPrevented`, attack declared/resolved/evaded, damaged, HP threshold,
moved, destroyed, marked, status applied, repair completed, faction changed.
**Mission layer:** 27 actions, 16 conditions, 28 event types.

## Commands — 9
`activateUnit` · `move` · `useAbility` · `trajectory` · `activateFixture` ·
`reorderActivations` · `setFacing` · `wait` · `endTurn`

### Does generic engine code depend on STATUS:ZERO-specific IDs?

**No, in `src/combat`, `src/reactions`, `src/mission`, `src/perception` and
`src/scene`** — enforced by two independent mechanisms:

- a **tripwire** in `scripts/run-tests.mjs` that fails the build if any whole
  string literal in those directories equals one of 60 forbidden content ids
  (operator names, frame ids, resource ids, ability ids, status ids). Verified
  each phase against a deliberately injected probe.
- `auditArchitecture()` (36 checks, PASS) which greps the engine half of
  `App.jsx` for React/DOM references and concrete content ids.

**`App.jsx` is the exception, by design and by debt.** Its engine half is
audited; its campaign half is *entirely* STATUS:ZERO-specific and is not
separated from the engine by any module boundary.

### Is gameplay content canonical or mirrored?

**Canonical.** `src/content/gameplay/*.json` is the single source of truth. The
game loads it, the Studio edits it, the editor's palette derives from it, and
`catalog.js` — which used to be a hand-maintained mirror — is now derived. What
is *not* canonical data: maps, encounters, terrain palette presentation, and
the entire campaign, all still literals in `App.jsx`.

### Where does authored gameplay data live?
`src/content/gameplay/` — 12 JSON registries plus their schema, validator,
format, exchange and arena modules.

### Where do missions / maps / scenes live?
Missions: `src/content/missions/*.json` (12). Scenes:
`src/content/scenes/*.json` (1). Maps: **inside mission files** for authored
missions, and inside `App.jsx`'s `MAPS` literal for campaign encounters.

### What remains in App.jsx that ideally should not?

In rough order of size and awkwardness:

1. **The entire campaign** (2,494 lines) — chapters, 13 missions, deployment
   rules, rewards, facilities, contacts. Nothing authors it and it is not
   canonical data.
2. **All 655 tests** — they need the engine's internals, but they make the file
   unnavigable.
3. **All 73 React components** — the presentation layer has no module boundary.
4. **`MAPS` and `ENCOUNTERS`** literals — content masquerading as source.
5. **`GAME_CONFIG` and `FORMULAS`** — arguably tuning data, not code.
6. **The engine itself** — event handlers, effects, AI, adapters. This is the
   part that *should* be in a module, and is the reason the other four are hard
   to move.

### Are frames represented as units/chassis or a separate registry?

**As units.** A frame is a `units.json` entry with a `classId`; an operator is
an `operators.json` entry naming a `chassis`. There is no separate frame or
platform registry. Class compatibility on equipment is the only frame-shaped
constraint.

### Is there multi-project support?

**No.** One hard-wired project. Content paths, the campaign, the theme and the
`window.STATUS_ZERO` global are all singular and named.

# Target Architecture

> **This section is TARGET, not current.** Nothing below is implemented unless
> it also appears in the sections above.

```
Generic SRPG Engine   +   Generic Authoring Tools   +   Project Content
```

A project should eventually be definable almost entirely by content folders:

```
projects/<project-id>/
├── campaign/        chapters, nodes, unlocks, rewards, economy
├── missions/        mission + map per file
├── scenes/
├── units/           chassis
├── classes/         optional: frames, platforms
├── equipment/  abilities/  statuses/  resources/  reactions/  fixtures/
├── factions/   ai/   progression/   loot/
├── assets/
├── theme/           palette, typography, framing
└── screens/         which UI surfaces exist and what they show
```

The generic runtime should know: **Unit · Ability · Effect · Condition ·
Status · Resource · Faction · Map · Mission · Scene · Campaign Node · Fixture ·
Reaction · Timeline · Perception.**

It should not know: **Vale · Nyx · Kell · Reyes · Aegis · Warner · Calder**, or
any STATUS:ZERO story concept.

That north star is already met inside `src/combat`, `src/reactions`,
`src/mission`, `src/perception` and `src/scene`. It is **not** met by
`App.jsx`, which is the single largest gap between current and target.

`docs/ENGINE_ANALYSIS.md` remains the authority on requirement-level intent and
is consistent with this target; it does not describe a different north star.

# Design Principle

> **Engine code defines reusable tactical primitives. Project content composes
> those primitives.**
>
> If a mechanic can be expressed with existing primitives, creating new content
> must not require source changes. Source changes are for genuinely new
> engine, editor or runtime primitives.

And the three rules the codebase already enforces:

> **Runtime state is not authored data.** Battle state serializes separately
> and is never written back into a registry.
>
> **Derived data is not authored data.** `catalog.js` derives from gameplay
> registries; the Studio's derived-value preview runs the engine's own stat
> pipeline rather than a copy.
>
> **Canonical content has stable IDs and deterministic serialization.**
> `serializeRegistryFile` produces byte-identical output for identical data,
> which is what makes a content diff reviewable.

# Known Architectural / Feature Debt

Verified against current code and the per-system docs. Items fixed in later
phases have been removed.

### Gameplay
- Subsystem impairment exists but **nothing in combat inflicts it**.
- Fixtures have **no detection** — hidden devices are revealed explicitly or not at all.
- One area shape per fixture; `triggerTiles` authored but unused; no arming delay.
- Trajectory: contacts are one target per leg; turn cost charged before the first tile; `slight` turns unreachable while diagonals are off.
- Propagation: one initial target; chain planned once per cast (deliberate, for preview parity); `nearestThenWeakest` policy unused.
- Interception: routes, fixture activations and reaction attacks do not declare — deliberate, to avoid reaction recursion. Movement redirect implemented but unused by content.
- Sequencing: one command at a time; `includeActive` and `hostileBarrier: "ignore"` implemented but unused.
- Support: no faction→unit transfer content; one operator carries a support economy.
- Nyx's stealth kit is unbuilt (STL-01).
- **Conditions cannot count nearby units.** Lone Wolf, outnumbered, formation
  and surrounded are the mechanics this blocks. Deliberately deferred: the
  counting itself is a dozen lines over existing helpers, but counting
  *hostiles* has to answer whether it reads the board or the asking faction's
  knowledge, and this project has an established position that a reaction must
  not become a detection oracle. That is a design decision, not a sweep item.
- No status trigger for the holder's own destruction, or for a status being
  removed. Both were evaluated and rejected for being unable to say
  unambiguously who the effect lands on; see `docs/TACTICAL_LANGUAGE.md`.

### AI
- **No planning for any advanced primitive**: cannot plan routes, chains, fixture placement, sequencing or support priority.
- Three weight-vector profiles only; no hunter, escort, civilian or ace behaviour (AI-01).
- No elite/ace phase behaviour — no retreat, loadout change or death refusal (BOS-01).

### Content / progression
- No crafting, vendors or black market. Materials exist and accumulate; nothing consumes them.
- **No mission-board replay UI.** The reward architecture supports repeat clears end to end and is tested through them, but the board filters completed missions out of `available`, so nothing offers one again.
- **13 Act One missions still author rewards in the `CAMPAIGN` literal**, adapted at one call site. Migration path documented in `docs/REWARDS.md`.
- No failure salvage; deliberately undefined rather than unimplemented.
- No difficulty concept anywhere, so no difficulty-scaled loot.
- Perks are one choice per operator, not trees (PRG-01).
- Facility capabilities are not attached to gameplay effects (HUB-01).
- Recruitment is a flag with no flow.
- Civilian/convoy behaviour is a tag and outcome facts, not behaviour (CIV-01).
- No disable/capture/surrender (CAP-01).

### Editor
- No campaign editor.
- No project creation or switching.
- Terrain presentation vocabulary (swatches, map characters, speakers, backgrounds) still hand-written in `catalog.js`.
- No in-app documentation or onboarding.

### Architecture
- **`App.jsx` is 46,682 lines** and holds the engine, all presentation, the whole campaign, map/encounter literals and every test.
- The campaign is a source literal, not authored data.
- No multi-project abstraction; `STATUS_ZERO` naming is baked into globals and storage keys.
- No large-map sectoring, culling or pathfinding budget (MAP-02).
- One map per battle; no submaps (SUB-01).

### Presentation
- No camera choreography or unit emphasis in scenes (CIN-01).
- Barks have no collision/cooldown rules (BARK-01).
- No theme layer.

### Testing / tooling
- All 716 tests live inside `App.jsx`.
- Browser acceptance requires a running dev server on :5173.
- Mission fixtures emit 28 standing warnings (scale/briefing/victory), which is noise that could mask a real one.

# Recommended Next Five Phases

Not started. Ordered by leverage.

### 1. Extract the engine from `App.jsx`
**Missing primitive:** none — this is a structural phase.
**Why now:** 44,656 lines is the single largest obstacle to every other item on
this list, and the only reason the campaign cannot become data. Nine phases
have proven the module pattern (`src/combat`, `src/reactions`, …); the engine
core is the last thing still living in the monolith.
**Risk: HIGH** — it touches everything, and the 655 tests live in the same file.
**Stress-tests:** every subsystem, the audit, and the tripwire's reach.

### 2. Campaign as authored data + campaign editor
**Missing primitive:** a campaign format, registry, validator and editor mode.
**Why now:** the campaign already exists and works — it is just in the wrong
place. Converting a *working* system to data is far safer than designing one.
Unblocks loot, progression and multi-project.
**Risk: MEDIUM** — the shape is known because the literal already defines it.
**Stress-tests:** the registry/schema/validate/exchange pattern at a new scale.

### 3. Equipment depth: state, durability, upgrades
**Missing primitive:** per-battle and persistent equipment state.
**Why now:** equipment is the thinnest major system and the natural home for
subsystem damage — which currently has nothing that inflicts it. The
status-driven impairment model already proved the honest way to express
degradation.
**Risk: LOW–MEDIUM** — additive; the registry and slot model already exist.
**Stress-tests:** subsystem impairment, support/repair, save/load, the Studio's
largest untouched registry.

### 4. AI behaviour and primitive planning
**Missing primitive:** a behaviour layer above weight vectors, plus planners
for routes, chains and placement.
**Why now:** every combat phase shipped with "the AI cannot plan this" as debt.
It is now the most-repeated debt item in the repository, and enemy operators
cannot use half the engine.
**Risk: MEDIUM–HIGH** — planning search has real performance and determinism
risk, and determinism is load-bearing everywhere.
**Stress-tests:** trajectory, propagation, fixtures, sequencing, perception,
and the determinism guarantees all at once.

### 5. Loot, salvage and the reward loop
**Missing primitive:** drop tables, salvage sources, vendor/exchange.
**Why now:** the persistence it needs already exists — equipment ownership,
frame condition, repair costs, supplies/funds/intel and facilities all persist
today. This was previously assumed to be blocked on progression persistence;
that assumption was wrong.
**Risk: LOW** — additive on top of a working campaign economy.
**Stress-tests:** campaign save/load, the equipment registry, mission results.
